Autonomy that lands where a mission survives.

Adinfinitium builds safety-first autonomy for landing and surface operations: software that picks where and when to land, flies the descent, and drives the rover, with a physics backup under every learned part.

What we believe

Physics first

Every learned model runs on top of a classical backup that always works on its own.

Test before flight

Thousands of simulated missions on real NASA terrain before anything flies.

Publish the evidence

Every number we show names its source file; anything generated is labelled as such.

Open models

All seven of our models are public on Hugging Face, with code on GitHub, so anyone can check and reproduce our benchmarks.

Physics backup first

Classical guidance always flies; learned models only correct it. An untrained model equals the backup.

Zero-regression gate

No self-improved model ships unless it loses no mission the current model or the backup lands.

Method & data

  • Terrain is observed: NASA LOLA 5 m/px polar tiles (PGDA); about 11 % of pixels are direct laser returns, the rest LOLA interpolation. Sun and Earth geometry: NASA/JPL SPICE DE440.
  • Every mission result is simulation: a simplified 3-DOF terminal-descent sim starting at about 2 km, on real terrain, with failures (engine-out, navigation drift, blind camera) injected. Not flight.
  • Pilot results: the same 2,304 missions per condition flown twice, by classic ZEM/ZEV guidance and by the pilot, on maps it never saw (runs/lander/benchmark.json). Site 23 final exam, flown once on the Mac mini: 97.95 % vs 97.95 % at normal rates (11 vs 11, p = 1.000), 96.29 % vs 96.48 % harsh (p = 0.875), a tie (lander_mini/runs/lander/benchmark_site23_final.json). The pooled unseen-map gain comes from Site 42 alone; Shoemaker and DM2 tie. The benchmark was replicated byte for byte on the Mac mini.
  • Models were trained on LOLA Sites 01, 04, 06, 07 and 20, validated on Site 11 and tested on Site 23, which was never used for training or model selection.
  • The altitude rail is an illustrative mission profile; the simulation itself covers the terminal ~2 km.
  • Landing ghosts: 960 simulated descents on Site 06 (classic, pilot and open loop for contrast) with failures injected at raised rates (engine-out 30 %, nav drift 30 %, blind camera 15 %): a visual, not a benchmark (runs/ghosts_web/lander_lola5m_site06.json).
  • Hazard labels come from 5 m slope maps, so rocks smaller than 5 m are unknown. The 3D lander and rover are illustrative models, not flight CAD.
  • Terrain: classical kriging (0 learned parameters) is the estimator we present: hide-and-check RMSE 0.94 m (Site 23) and 1.11 m (Site 42) against 1.83 m and 2.80 m for our 49.9 M-parameter diffusion network (records/weak_flags/REPORT_terrain.md). The network is an experiment; anything it makes is labelled GENERATED — NOT OBSERVED and is never used alone for a safety claim.
  • Mac mini hand-off (15 Nov 2026, data/handoff_macmini_1115): an overnight run trained 60 M hazard, 38 M light and 50 M terrain variants and a rover driver, then froze them with checksums; later work is in pre-registered addenda (macbook/records/SUBMISSION_SUMMARY.md). These variants are different models from the ones on our product pages; their numbers are never mixed with ours in one claim.
  • Rover: two learned drivers (PPO and imitation, 11,377,354 parameters each) failed; the scripted brain v3.1 returned 33.7 % and lost 53.9 % of rovers on four untouched sites LM2–LM5 under the strict rule (any flat battery = lost), against 17.9 % and 81.8 % for the naive planner (records/rover_v31/tables.md).
  • Lander self-improvement on 15 Nov: 0 of 7 candidates passed the zero-regression gate on Site 11; the shipped pilot is unchanged (lander_mini/runs/lander/rsi_changelog.md). End-to-end test on unseen site DM1: 65 of 65 integration checks passed (records/final/e2e_result_final_v31.json); an integration test, not a benchmark.
  • All test sites in the hand-off (Site 23, Site 42, DM1, LM7, NPA–NPD, LM2–LM5) have now been used; any future score on them is "previously seen", and a new held-out test needs new sites.
  • The cursors in "How it was built" are a dramatisation; the code lines (guidance.py 32–49, pilot.py 23 and 104–105, hazard/features.py 36 and 46–56), images, hazard F1 scores and commit counts are real. Commit counts: git log --all via tools/export_build_log.py; "with an AI coding agent" = authored or co-authored by one.
  • Pilot output: thrust = clip(classic + 0.3 × r, −1, 1), r in [−1, 1] per axis; engine tilt and throttle limits still apply. Checkpoint d53a162df9 has no learned authority rule, so the correction always applies in full.
  • Pilot inputs: a 3 × 32 × 32 crop under the lander (slope ÷ 12°, slope-rule hazard belief, relative height), 21 observations and the 3 classic commands; 66 tokens × 384, 8 layers, 8 heads, 14,842,567 parameters. The patches shown are real crops at randomly sampled positions, not a flown path (assets/img/pilot_patches.json). The network drawing shows only the strongest sampled weights.
  • Benchmark, normal failure rates: classic 97.66 % vs pilot 98.39 %, +0.74 points, 95 % CI [+0.2, +1.3], exact McNemar p = 0.019; 32 missions only the pilot landed, 15 only classic. Harsh (3× failures): 96.31 % vs 96.92 %, +0.61 points, p = 0.18, not significant (runs/lander/benchmark.md).
  • Planner: on held-out Site 23 the learned set planner (12.2 %) lost to the Navigator ranking (12.8 %), so the Navigator ranking ships.
  • Real-mission comparison: external figures are from the cited JAXA, ISRO and Science News pages; ours is a simplified 3-DOF simulation of the last ~2 km, not flight.
  • Rover: run cautious_15 from runs/ghosts/rover_lola5m_site23_2027-09-01.json, the only exported run that reached an ice target and returned; battery and sunlight from re-running that seeded run. On the harder date 2027-03-01 the same planner returned 0 of 32 rovers (runs/rover/eval.json). Rover and lander are drawn 16× size in the overview. Ice targets are permanently shadowed crater edges: dark and cold, not confirmed ice.
  • Training curves ("Watch it learn"): landing pilot vs classic on validation Site 11 from results_pc/extracted/runs/pilot.log (the shipped checkpoint d53a162df9 is the update-40 peak; later updates did not beat it); hazard F1 per epoch from runs/hazard/train_log.jsonl; light skyline error from runs/light/train_log.jsonl; Dream-RSI generations from results_pc/extracted/planner_dream/history.jsonl. Terrain has too few logged points and is left out.
  • Evolution (NEAT) visual: sample data (assets/data/neat_training.json, "placeholder": true). The swarm is designed and tested but has not run; no generation, fitness or champion files exist. The pilot was trained by PPO (swarm/README.md; data/handoff_macmini_1115/REVIEW.md §3).
  • Landing descents in "Watch it learn": 960 runs, 320 per policy (no guidance, classic, AI pilot) with the same starts, from runs/ghosts_web/lander_lola5m_site06.json; failures injected at raised rates, a visual, not a benchmark.

How we used AI

The team wrote the software together with AI coding agents; cinematic video and imagery are AI-generated with Google Gemini.

Cinematic video and imagery: AI-generated with Google Gemini

Learned models (pilot, hazard U-Net, light skyline, planner) are trained by us on NASA data and checked against physics backups on held-out sites. AI-generated media is decorative only and never used as data.
