Adinfinitium Pilot

Landing and surface autonomy that runs a physics backup first.

Join the waitlist Read the research

What it does

Lands

Flies the terminal descent: a learned correction on top of classic ZEM/ZEV guidance, steering away from steep ground.

Sees hazards

Turns sparse laser heights into a slope-hazard map of the landing zone.

Knows the light

Predicts the terrain skyline, then Sun and Earth visibility for every hour.

Drives

Moves the rover to a shadowed-crater edge and back, waiting for light when it must.

How it works

Four parts, each with a physics backup that runs first. If a learned part is unavailable or worse, its backup flies. Open a card for its diagram, data and test numbers.

What it senses, what it controls

The sensor suite is modelled on Chandrayaan-3's. The pilot outputs three thrust corrections on top of the classic command; the engines do the rest.

Next: an evolution swarm.

Today's pilot was trained by PPO (reinforcement learning). Next on our roadmap is an evolution swarm: small networks, each a correction on top of classic guidance, fly simulated landings and the fittest of every generation breed the next. The swarm is designed and tested but has not run yet; the animation below uses sample values, not results.

Rover driver.

Two learned drivers were trained and both failed (0.0 % and 1.2 % of rovers returned, against 9.3 % for a simple planner). The brain we present, v3.1, is scripted: on four untouched sites it returned 33.7 % of rovers and lost 53.9 % under the strict rule, against 17.9 % and 81.8 % for the best simple planner. Specifications

Inputs
32 × 32 egocentric camera crop (slope, hazard belief, relative height); 21 observations (13 sensors + an 8-direction hazard ring); the 3 classic guidance commands
Outputs
3 thrust corrections, added to the classic command at scale 0.3 and clipped; 5 engines (4 outer + 1 centre), 800 N each, throttle 40–100 %, thrust tilt up to 45°
Parameters
Pilot 14,842,567 · hazard U-Net 1,944,337 · light skyline CNN 32,737
Update rate
Descent control every 0.1 s (10 Hz) in simulation; light and rover decisions once per hour
Backups
Classic ZEM/ZEV guidance · slope rule (gap-fill, slope ≥ 12°) · direct ray-cast of the terrain · rule-based rover drivers
Training data
NASA LOLA 5 m terrain, Sites 01, 04, 06, 07, 20; validated on Site 11; tested on Site 23 and unseen maps
