# Rover v3.1 plan (pre-registered)

Sun 15 Nov 2026, written about 13:50 IST, before any v3.1 code ran and before any v3.1 evaluation number exists. Branch `rover-v3.1` (from `rover-v3` 6742c7b). After-freeze experiment; nothing in the frozen main run changes; no simulator physics changes.

## 0. What is already known (and what this plan may use)

- Rover v3 (scripted planner + scripted executor + gated self-learning) on six fresh sites: lenient rule returned 59.6%, lost 0.0%; STRICT rule (an empty battery anywhere is a lost rover) returned 42.0%, lost 56.7%. Best planner strict 29.8% / 69.4%.
- Where v3's strict losses come from (weak-flags addendum, fresh sites): 4,801 rovers sat at the lander with an empty battery and never made the trip; 2,164 returned but had an empty battery earlier while waiting at the lander for light. The lander is often dark for more than 50 h; a rover idles 50 h on a full battery.
- v3 has ONE place to wait: the lander. It never looks for lit ground nearby. That is the gap this plan addresses.
- Wrong light forecast (12 h late): v3 lenient returned 30.9%, lost 27.3%; self-learning accepted nothing (no proposal type for a time-shifted forecast).
- Self-learning: 9 of 72 rounds accepted, all in round 1.
- CONTAMINATION: Site23, Site42 and DM1, LM7, NPA, NPB, NPC, NPD have been seen (v3 final evaluation and the weak-flags addendum, including the late-light definition of 12 h). The design below is informed by those numbers. They are therefore NOT the primary test here.

A diagnosis sub-agent measures, on development and validation sites only, how much of the strict loss is unavoidable (section 2). The brain design below was fixed before its numbers came in. If the diagnosis shows the losses are unavoidable, the new candidates will simply not be chosen, and the report will say so.

## 1. What changes in the brain (and why)

All scripted; no neural network; no simulator change. The frozen v3 behaviour stays available as `Theta(v31=False)` and is the "v3" row of every table (it must reproduce the frozen v3 counts on the previously seen sites exactly; if it does not, that is reported and the v3 row is labelled NOT REPRODUCED).

1. **Stay-alive route (new planner component).** For each rover, a forward search over a grid of positions around the rover (36 m spacing = one flat hourly move, +-12 nodes = +-432 m, 4 directions, steep edges take 2-4 hours or are blocked) and over every hour of the remaining episode, computing the highest battery level the rover can have at each (place, hour) without ever being empty, from the belief light forecast and belief physics. Reading it backwards gives a route: where to be at each hour to keep the battery above empty (drive to lit ground, sit, move on when the light moves).
2. **New candidate plans** (added to v3's 36, none removed): "shelter only" (follow the stay-alive route to the end of the episode) and "shelter, then go" (follow the stay-alive route until hour w, for each of v3's 16 waits, then drive to the target and back to the lander, with or without charging stops). The route for wait w ends at the place with the most battery left after subtracting the dark-driving energy from that place to the target (a fixed heuristic; the exact roll-out decides which candidate wins). Every candidate is rolled forward in the rover's own model exactly as in v3, so in an unchanged world prediction = outcome.
3. **Choice rule, strict first.** Rank of a candidate (higher wins; ties broken as in v3: returned, reached target, model reward, then list order):
   - rank 3: reaches the target, returns, and the battery is never empty anywhere (a strict return);
   - rank 2: the battery is never empty anywhere until the end of the episode (rover alive, no completed trip);
   - rank 1: v3's rule (not dead under the simulator's lenient rule; includes "return with a flat battery at the lander" and "sit at the lander");
   - a plan predicted to die under the simulator's rule is never followed.
   Consequence fixed here, before any number: when a rover has no strict return, v3.1 prefers staying alive (rank 2) over a return that needs a flat battery at the lander (rank 1). So **v3.1's lenient "returned" may be LOWER than v3's**. That is intended: a flat battery next to the lander is not counted as a success in v3.1. Both rules are reported for every method.
   - By construction in an unchanged world (exact model, v3's candidates are a subset): v3.1 strict returned >= v3 strict returned and v3.1 strict lost <= v3 strict lost for every rover set. Checked on validation (section 4).
   - The self-learned `margin` applies as in v3 (minimum battery away from the lander) and, for ranks 3 and 2, to the minimum battery anywhere.
4. **Executor.** One new mode: follow a list of waypoints by hour (steer by plain distance with the same slope sensing; hold when within reach of the waypoint). Replanning every 6 h as in v3 (battery or position off the plan), now also recomputing the stay-alive route.
5. **Self-learning layer (Dream-RSI), fixes and additions:**
   - new proposal type **light forecast shifted in time**: from its own telemetry (hour, place, measured solar power) the rover fits the shift k in {-36, -33, ..., +36} h (and the solar factor jointly) that best explains the readings; proposed only if k != 0 and the squared error at k is below half the error at k = 0 with at least 50 samples; the proposal is a belief whose forecast is shifted by k;
   - new proposal type **battery capacity <- measured** (the rover reads its own full-battery level; proposed if it differs from the belief by more than 5%);
   - **memory of rejected proposals**: v3 sent only the best imagined proposal to the real gate each round and had no memory, so a rejected proposal could be sent again and block the others. v3.1 does not resend a proposal that the gate rejected for the same belief and sends the best imagined proposal not yet rejected. (Whether this was the reason nothing was accepted after round 1 is checked by the diagnosis agent from the saved v3 rounds; reported either way.)
   - **gate (at least as strict as v3's)**: same form and thresholds (128 paired real episodes on the same starts; accept only if mean gain > 0 and > 2 standard errors, no fewer returned, no more lost). Scoring changes to the strict rule because the brain's objective is now strict: per-episode score = simulator reward, minus the simulator's own death penalty (50) for a rover whose battery was empty within 20 m of the lander (not otherwise penalised), minus the return bonus for a return that had an empty battery; "returned" and "lost" in the gate are the strict counts; and in addition the simulator's own (lenient) died count must not increase. Imagination uses the same strict score.
   - NOT built (time): detecting a forecast shift inside a single episode and replanning at once. The shift is learned between batches of episodes only.

## 2. Diagnosis (development and validation sites only)

Under the strict rule on v3: split the losses into (a) kept at the lander, battery empties there; (b) returned, but battery was empty earlier; (c) empty away from the lander. Unavoidable share: (i) a provable bound: a rover is certainly lost if, even collecting at every hour the best sunlight of any cell it could possibly have reached by then (36 m per hour) while paying only the idle load, its battery reaches empty; (ii) the stay-alive search of section 1 (an estimate of what is achievable); (iii) what v3.1 actually achieves in exact roll-outs (a lower bound on avoidable). Also from the saved v3 self-learning rounds: why nothing was accepted after round 1, and why dusty panels rarely improved. Reported in plain numbers; if most losses are unavoidable the report says so.

## 4. Validation (go / no-go; no parameter is selected)

There is no tunable to select: every constant in section 1 is fixed here. Validation = 6 sites x 4 dates, 128 rovers per world, seed 4321, unchanged worlds, v3.1 and v3 and the three planners:
- G1 no crash; G2 prediction = outcome for at least 99% of v3.1 rovers in every world;
- G3 in every world: v3.1 strict returned >= v3 strict returned, v3.1 strict lost <= v3 strict lost, v3.1 lenient died <= v3 lenient died;
- G4 the `v31=False` path gives the same counts as the frozen v3 code on the same worlds and starts.
A failure of G1-G4 is a code defect: fix the code (not the constants), log it, rerun. If it cannot be fixed in time, the final evaluation still runs once and the report says which check failed. A changed-world and self-learning smoke test runs on development site Site01 only (crashes and timing). Validation numbers are reported as validation, never as a score.
One allowed fallback, decided on validation only: if the stay-alive search makes a world take more than 4 minutes at 128 rovers, the grid radius is reduced from 12 to 8 nodes (compute reason only), recorded in an amendment before the final launch.

## 5. The single final evaluation (launched once; a script that refuses a second launch)

Mac mini, CPU only, at most 7 busy processes, 1 thread each. Same worlds, starts, seeds and episode counts for every method. Strict AND lenient reported everywhere.
- **Stage A (PRIMARY)**: untouched sites x 4 dates x 512 rovers, seed 1234: v3.1, v3, naive, cautious, cautious + wait for light, safe harbour only.
- **Stage B (previously seen)**: the six earlier fresh sites and Site23 + Site42, same protocol.
- **Stage C (changed worlds, before learning)**: untouched sites, date 2027-06-01, 256 rovers, seed 1234; the rover is not told: blocked route, dusty panels (both as in v3), late light (true sunlight = forecast delayed by 12 h, the weak-flags definition), and one new unrelated change, weak battery (capacity x 0.7, synthetic). v3.1, v3, planners (not told and told).
- **Stage D (self-learning)**: untouched sites, 2027-06-01; situations in this order: for each site: late light, dusty panels, blocked route, unchanged; then weak battery for each site. v3.1 brain with the v3.1 layer; 2 rounds; gate 128 paired real episodes; imagination 64 episodes; before / after on 256 separate score starts (never used to choose anything); seeds by the v3 rule. Wall-clock cap 30 minutes; situations not finished at the cap are reported as not finished. Label per situation by the v3 rule on the strict score: IMPROVED (>= 1 accepted and gain > 2 SE), WORSE (gain < -2 SE), else NO CHANGE.
- Cut order if behind schedule (decided before launch, written in an amendment): first weak battery (C and D), then nothing else.
- Intervals: site-level bootstrap (resampling sites, 10,000 draws, seed 0) as in v3; with 4 sites they are rough, so per-site signs are given too.

## 6. Success criteria

- **PRIMARY (stage A, strict rule): PASS if** v3.1 strict lost minus v3 strict lost is below 0 with its site-bootstrap 95% interval entirely below 0, AND v3.1 strict returned minus v3 strict returned >= 0, AND v3.1 lenient died <= v3 lenient died. Otherwise FAIL.
- Size statement (not a pass rule): the report gives the absolute change in strict lost and strict returned; a gain under 2 points is to be called "small" in the report.
- S1: the same rule on each previously seen set (no interval for Site23 + Site42).
- S2: lenient returned v3.1 vs v3 is reported, not a criterion (expected equal or lower, section 1.3).
- S3 (stage C, per change): v3.1 strict lost <= v3 strict lost and v3.1 strict returned >= v3 strict returned on the site mean. MET / NOT MET per change. Also reported against the best planner told and not told.
- S4 (stage D): per change (late light, dusty panels, blocked route): more than half of the finished situations IMPROVED and none WORSE; unchanged: none WORSE. MET / NOT MET / NOT EVALUABLE (fewer than 3 finished).
- **On failure**: the numbers are reported as they are; v3 stays the recommended brain; nothing is rerun or adjusted after a test number has been seen.

## Amendment 1 (13:52 IST; after the independent plan review and the diagnosis numbers on development/validation sites; before any v3.1 code ran; no test number exists)

Known when written: the plan review (`records/agents/rover31-plan-reviewer.md`: 1 blocker, 6 should-fix) and the diagnosis on development + validation sites (`records/rover_v31/diagnosis.md`: v3 strict lost 56.1% of 4,608 rovers; 71% of the losses are rovers that went flat at the lander without a trip, 15% "returned" after being flat, 13% simulator deaths, all at Site04; fully proven unavoidable 8.1% of the losses; an optimistic estimate says about 34% of the losses had some stay-alive plan). The brain design of section 1 is unchanged by these numbers. Time correction: the plan was written 13:38-13:40 (commit 13:40), not 13:50; the fetch of LM2-LM5 finished 13:39:44; their worlds (terrain, light, hazard map; no rover episode) were built by 13:49; all four have 16 PSR-edge targets, so all four are in the test.

1. **PRIMARY rule tightened (blocker).** Because v3.1 cannot be worse than v3 on strict lost / strict returned in an unchanged world if the code is right, the old rule could pass with one saved rover. New rule: **PASS** only if, on stage A, strict lost falls by at least 2.0 points on the site mean, falls at 3 or more of the 4 sites, the site-bootstrap interval of the difference is below 0, strict returned does not fall, and lenient died does not rise. **PASS-SMALL ("no material gain")** if strict lost falls but by less than 2.0 points or at fewer than 3 sites. Otherwise **FAIL**. The report must say: "v3.1 cannot score below v3 under the strict rule in unchanged worlds if the code is right; stage A only shows how much lit ground within reach exists under a perfect forecast; stages C and D are the only tests of a wrong model."
2. **When no candidate is rank 1 or better** (every plan is predicted to die under the simulator's rule), v3.1 follows exactly what v3 follows (safe harbour).
3. **Gate wording corrected.** The v3.1 gate has the same form and thresholds as v3's (128 paired episodes, gain > 0 and > 2 SE, count conditions) but the gain is measured on the strict score, so it is NOT comparable in strictness on the gain test. Count conditions, all required: strict returned no fewer, strict lost no more, lenient died no more, lenient returned no fewer (the last one added here so that every count condition of v3's gate is kept). For each accepted change the report says whether v3's original gate (simulator reward gain > 2 SE) would also have accepted it. A situation whose strict lost count rises on the score starts is labelled WORSE whatever the gain.
4. **Forecast-shift proposal**: the fit grid is every whole hour from -36 to +36 (not steps of 3 h). The late-light test (12 h late) is the definition already seen on the fresh sites, so it shows "recovers a time shift", not "handles wrong forecasts in general". Added test: **light 8 h early** (true sunlight = forecast advanced by 8 h; the last 8 hours repeat the last frame), stages C and D. Weak battery has a dedicated proposal type, so no change WITHOUT a dedicated proposal type is tested; the report must not call the tested changes "unrelated to what the layer was built for".
5. **Stage D**: order: for each site: late light, dusty panels, blocked route, unchanged; then early light for each site; then weak battery for each site. 7 situations at a time, 1 thread each. S4 denominator is all 4 sites; a situation not finished at the cap counts as NO CHANGE. **Cut order** (before launch only, by amendment): weak battery (C and D) first, then early light in D. Nothing is cut after launch. Finished worlds are never rerun; a crashed world is reported as crashed (one rerun allowed only for a machine failure, logged).
6. **Sites are neighbours.** LM2-LM5 footprints lie 10-30 km from each other (LM2 and LM3 footprints overlap; LM5's footprint is 4 km from seen site LM7's). The 5 km centre windows used here do not overlap each other or any used window (centres at least 10 km apart). The four sites are not independent samples of the Moon; the interval is rough.
7. **Baseline.** The v3 row is frozen v3 (`v31=False`), not the weak-flags "strict-aware reserve" variant, which was about 1 point better than frozen v3 on the six fresh sites (strict lost 55.5% against 56.7%). The report states this. The `v31=False` harness is checked on validation (G4) and its stage B counts must equal the frozen v3 counts (7,327 returned, 0 died of 12,288 on the six fresh sites).
8. **Reporting.** Every table gives both rules for every method; the fall in strict lost is split into "new strict returns" and "alive, no trip" (with the battery left at the end for the latter); a strict number is never compared with a lenient one; "alive" means until hour 300 only. The diagnosis bound is reported as a range (proven unavoidable ... estimate), never as one "avoidable share". Code changes after this point: only for a failed G1-G4 or a crash, each logged.
